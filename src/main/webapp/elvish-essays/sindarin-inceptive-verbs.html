Sindarin Inceptive Verbs

by Paul Strack (2026)

NOTE: This article is deeply speculative, based on very thin evidence. Everything in it should be used with caution.

Introduction

It is fairly well-known that Sindarin and Noldorin had two distinct past forms for a-verbs: a transitive past tense -ant and an intransitive past tense -as(t). See the discussion of the past tenses of transitive derived verbs and intransitive derived verbs for further details. It is known that the transitive past suffix -ant probably arose by analogy with half-strong transitive verbs like [N.] tangad(a)- “make firm” with past forms like tangant (PE17/44). The likeliest source for the intransitive past suffix -as is a similar analogy from intransitive inceptive verb like primitive ✶tankas-, whose past forms used suffixal -ansē which would become -ass(e) > -as in modern Sindarin.

For this to be the case, the inceptives would need to be a fairly large class of verbs in Sindarin, but we have no examples in Tolkien’s currently-published writings. This document attempts to deduce what these inceptive verbs might look like.

Primitive s-inceptives

Tolkien described inceptive verbs in some detail in Common Eldarin: Noun Structure (EVS2) from the early 1950s. One type of inceptive he described was s-inceptives:

To this class belonged also the inceptives formed with s. This s was added direct to the stem of adjectives, but in the case of verbal stems required an “extension”. Thus tañkās- “become firm, settle down”; niñkwis- “(begin to) grow pale”; but kalas- “begin to shine, get light”; talat- > taltas- “begin to slip down”. These verbs were originally conjugated like TALAT-bases; but later their aorist and present forms coalesced in -sjā̆ (niñkwisjā̆); but their past retained the form -nsĭ/e (niñkwinsē). The majority of these verbs surviving in later Eldarin were from adjectival stems (PE22/135).

Outside of this note, the only concrete example of this verb class we have is Q. tankarya “*become firm” along with what appears to be a past form tankanse and a verb suffix -arya, all appearing in notes from 1964 (PE17/75). These forms are consistent with the system described in EVS2.

On the Sindarin side, primitive verb *kalas- would have a past form kalansē, which in Sindarin would produce calas “began to shine” since ancient ns became ss in Sindarin. Inflected pasts like kalansē-ni would become calassen or possibly calessin/celessin if ancient long ē became i; see the the entry on Sindarin inflected pasts for how a joining vowel of e or i would influence past forms. See also attested past forms rithessin “*I tried” vs. uninflected past rithas (PE17/167).

Present-tense inflections are less clear. It depends on whether or not the inceptive suffix used for Sindarin present-tense forms was -s or variant -sjā̆. If we assume the suffix is -s, then ancient uninflected aorist and present tense forms *kalasi and *kalasā would coalesce into kalahǝ > cala, indistinguishable for any other a-verb. However, inflected kalasi-ni would develop: kelehin(i) > kelein > celein > celain, a rather exotic present tense form. The closest attested analog I can find is [N.] noen “wise” with (archaic) plural nœin, derived respectively *nusina > nohen(a) > noen and *nusini > nœhin(i) > nœin [> nein] (PE22/40).

On the other hand, if the primitive present was kalasyā, its uninflected form would develop to keleꜧ(i) > celeich > celaich. Tolkien seems to have been rather uncertain about the medial development of sj. In notes from 1969, he first indicated medially ꜧ became χ [ch] (PE23/135 note #6) but then indicated it vocalized medially in a way similar to kt > kth > ı̯þ, that is ꜧ > ı̯χ [ich].
